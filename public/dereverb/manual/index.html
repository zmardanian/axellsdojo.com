MANUAL · SMALL-ROOM DE-REVERB

Axell De-Reverb: getting started and every control

Take the room out of your vocal. Keep the voice. This page explains every control, with the plugin's own screens. Back to Axell De-Reverb.

Getting started

  1. Install. Windows: run the Axell Signature Suite installer (or copy Axell De-Reverb.vst3 to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3). Linux: unpack the archive and run ./install.sh.
  2. Rescan. Let your DAW rescan its VST3 plugins; Axell De-Reverb appears under the vendor Axell Yobag.
  3. Insert it on the track or bus it is made for, and start from a factory preset close to your material.
  4. Listen, then adjust the few controls described below; A/B compares two settings without losing either.

What it does

The de-reverb of the Axell suite. It removes the room from a close vocal:

  • the tail of a bare bedroom or a bathroom;
  • the early reflections and the 350-600 Hz box of a wardrobe booth, a closet or a car, which most de-reverbs leave alone because they were built for long tails in big rooms.
  • A trained network runs on your CPU (no internet, nothing to install). It predicts, for every moment and frequency, how much of the sound is your voice and how much is the room. Below 4.5 kHz it also undoes the room with a short complex filter over several frames, so it can remove reflections that arrive within a few milliseconds of the voice.
    • Training: rap and spoken word first (2.0: dense takes with 0-120 ms between phrases). 80 speaking voices (women and men, 11 accents) and 16 singers, in 4,000 simulated small rooms (booths, closets, cars, bedrooms, bathrooms) and 7 measured rooms.
    • 10 % of the training had no room at all: a dry take must come out as it went in.
  • REDUCTION 0-100 %. The default 85 % was the best balance on the benchmark (DECISIONS D9). At 0 % the output is the input, delayed.
  • LISTEN: REMOVED plays only what is taken away (processed + removed = the input).
  • OUTPUT -24 to +24 dB. In/out meters, a REMOVED meter and a room history.
  • Presets: Natural (85 %), Clean (100 %), Gentle (60 %). User presets and A/B.
  • STUDIO / LIVE (3.0): STUDIO looks 32 ms ahead (latency 2560 samples at 48 kHz, 53 ms, reported and compensated) - for mixing. LIVE has no latency at all (48 kHz; the resamplers' 2-3 ms at other rates): a causal network applied as a minimum-phase filter, for hearing yourself without the room while you record. LIVE removes about two thirds of what STUDIO removes (3.7 vs 5.7 dB of room on 48 held-out examples, ViSQOL 4.00 vs 4.13) and harms the voice no more.
  • Any sample rate: 44.1 to 96 kHz. The network runs at 48 kHz, with exact-latency resampling, and the band above it passes untouched.
