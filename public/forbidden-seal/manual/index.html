Axell Forbidden Seal: getting started and every control

Saturation from physics. Your voice never passes a circuit. This page explains every control, with the plugin's own screens. Back to Axell Forbidden Seal.

Getting started

  1. Install. Windows: run the Axell Signature Suite installer (or copy Axell Forbidden Seal.vst3 to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3). Linux: unpack the archive and run ./install.sh.
  2. Rescan. Let your DAW rescan its VST3 plugins; Axell Forbidden Seal appears under the vendor Axell Yobag.
  3. Insert it on the track or bus it is made for, and start from a factory preset close to your material.
  4. Listen, then adjust the few controls described below; A/B compares two settings without losing either.

What it does

The saturation of the Axell suite: eight circuits modelled from physics, and - new in 4.0 - decoupled saturation: the voice never passes a circuit; what each circuit adds is split into its squash and its new harmonics, and the harmonics are placed where they flatter a voice - out of the vowels' range where a saturator turns mids into a box, out of the fizz band, into weight and bite. Your highs stay silky; your esses stay out of the grit.

  • Eight styles: TAPE (magnetic hysteresis), TRIODE, PENTODE (push-pull), CONSOLE (transformer into class A), GERMANIUM (a starving vintage transistor), IRON (transformer core), DIODE, FOLD - each with a CHARACTER control (tape speed, tube bias, class A to B, core size...).
  • BANDS: up to three phase-coherent bands, each with its own circuit, drive and mix.
  • ESS GUARD: the suite's sibilance network finds the esses and keeps them out of the saturation, at their natural balance to the vowels - no fizz on the S sounds, however hard you drive the rest.
  • DYNAMICS: the drive follows the level - more bite on the accents, or thicker quiet parts.
  • FOCUS (4.0): where the colour lands - tuned per circuit and per drive on a real voice against a respected tape machine emulation; 0 % = the classic circuit, all of its colour wherever it falls.
  • VOICING (4.0): the seal's tone - a touch of weight, the honk range eased, a breath of air; off = the voice exactly as it came in.
  • INPUT + LEARN: plays your take for 6 seconds and sets the level so every style and preset behaves as designed, whatever level the take was recorded at.
  • AUTO keeps the loudness while you turn DRIVE, so you compare colour, not volume.
  • QUALITY from Eco (2x) to Extreme (16x) oversampling, and Live (4x) with about 4 samples of latency for recording through it.

The face (4.0): DRIVE is a katana drawn out of its scabbard - the blade heats with the drive, its temper line ripples with what the circuit makes; every other control is a brush ensō that closes with its value; switches are hanko seals; Miyamoto Musashi's painting seal 二天 (Niten) stamped over 禁, glowing with HEAT; the sections named after his five rings (地 earth, 水 water, 火 fire, 風 wind, 空 void). 660 x 350 at 100 %, resizable to 200 %. Every control: drag (Shift: fine), mouse wheel, double-click to the default, right-click to type a value.

More screens: tape and its hysteresis loop, triode, push-pull pentode, the wavefolder.

Before the seal (what distorts): INPUT + LEARN, LOW CUT + THUMP (a resonant bump at the low cut), TONE (a tilt). After the seal: HIGH CUT (6 dB/oct, or STEEP: 30 dB/oct), AIR.
