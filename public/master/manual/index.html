MANUAL · MASTERING LIMITER

Axell Master: getting started and every control

Loud, clean masters that survive the streaming codecs. This page explains every control, with the plugin's own screens. Back to Axell Master.

Getting started

  1. Install. Windows: run the Axell Signature Suite installer (or copy Axell Master.vst3 to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3). Linux: unpack the archive and run ./install.sh.
  2. Rescan. Let your DAW rescan its VST3 plugins; Axell Master appears under the vendor Axell Yobag.
  3. Insert it on the track or bus it is made for, and start from a factory preset close to your material.
  4. Listen, then adjust the few controls described below; A/B compares two settings without losing either.

What it does

The mastering limiter of the Axell suite: an oversampled soft clipper into a true-peak limiter, with a loudness meter to the broadcast standard, delivery targets with LEARN, a level-matched COMPARE, psychoacoustically shaped dither for the export, and (2.0) a check of the master through the real streaming codecs, with SAFE to keep a loud master from clipping after YouTube or Spotify encode it. Built for rap, trap and boom-bap masters, and transparent enough for acoustic music.

GAIN drives the mix into the processing. LEARN sets it for you: play the song, press LEARN, then APPLY. GAIN moves to hit the chosen target. A second pass lands within a few tenths of an LU.

CLIPPER shaves the shortest peaks (kicks, snares) before the limiter, so the limiter works less and pumps less.

  • CLIP sets how much of the peak reduction is clipping.
  • SHAPE runs from soft (rounded) to hard (flat).
  • 16x oversampling is the default (1x / 2x / 4x / 8x selectable). Even a hard clip of a bright mix stays clean: aliasing is below -99 dBc.
  • The live curve shows the clipper's transfer and your loudest recent peak.

LIMITER is a true-peak limiter (ceiling -24 to 0 dBTP, release 1-1000 ms, look-ahead 0.5-5 ms, stereo link). It has four program-dependent release styles:

  • TRANSPARENT (default): natural.
  • PUNCHY: drums keep their snap.
  • LOUD: the densest.
  • SAFE: the slowest.

A lone peak recovers fast and dense passages slowly. A bass-aware hold keeps the gain steady through a bass note's cycle: 40-50 Hz bass at 6 dB of limiting measures -139 dB THD+N in Transparent and Safe.

LOUDNESS follows ITU-R BS.1770-4 / EBU R128. It shows:

  • integrated (large), short-term, momentary;
  • loudness range (EBU Tech 3342);
  • true peak (8x, twice the standard's minimum);
  • PLR;
  • the input's integrated loudness.

Targets: -14 LUFS (Spotify / YouTube / Tidal), -16 (Apple Music), -9 (club / CD), -23 (EBU R128), -24 (ATSC A/85). The history shows the last seconds: peaks in and out, short-term loudness against the target, and clipper and limiter gain reduction.

OUTPUT:

  • VOICE HOLD (3.0, for a vocal or vocal bus): in the voice's pauses the limiter holds its gain instead of releasing, so the drive does not lift the room and the breaths (Vocal Peak Catcher +12 dB on a real take: pauses +8.1 -> +2.7 dB re the voice). On in the vocal presets; leave it off on a mix or a beat.
  • ESS HOLD (4.0, for a vocal or vocal bus): through each ess the limiter holds the word's gain instead of releasing, so the drive does not push the esses above the vowels (Vocal Peak Catcher +12 dB, 28 real takes: esses over their word +2.76 -> +0.40 dB, vowels within 0.02 dB). On in the vocal presets; leave it off on a mix or a beat.
  • COMPARE plays the unprocessed mix at the master's loudness, so you hear what the processing does and not just "louder". In the audit it matched within 0.03 LU.
  • DITHER for the final export: 24-bit or 16-bit TPDF, or 16-bit shaped. The shaped mode uses the 9th-order F-weighted minimally audible filter (Wannamaker, JAES 1992), which is 22 dB quieter than plain TPDF at 2-5 kHz.

STREAMING CODECS (2.0). A -1 dBTP ceiling protects the file, but the services re-encode it, and a lossy codec's decoded peaks land higher than the master's. Measured on real rap masters at -9 LUFS, 1.0's -1 dBTP masters decoded at up to +1.1 dBTP (Vorbis 160, Spotify) and +0.6 (AAC), with clipped samples in every song.

  • The check: while the master plays, it is encoded and decoded with the real Opus 160 kbps (YouTube) and Vorbis 160 kbps (Spotify) libraries, each at four frame offsets. Where the frames fall moves the decoded peak by up to 0.9 dB, and the service's framing is unknown.
  • Display: the decoded true peak and clipped samples since the last change. Green = safe, amber = within 0.3 dB of full scale, red = clips.
  • Threading: it runs on its own thread and never touches the audio. On / off with the STREAMING CODECS switch.
  • SAFE: lowers the ceiling until both codecs decode at -0.3 dBTP or below. Play the song again and press again if offered: a lower ceiling means denser limiting, so it usually takes 1-3 presses.
    • Verified on 9 loud masters: 0 clipped samples with Opus, Vorbis 160 / 320, FDK AAC 256 and MP3 320 at 6 frame offsets each.
    • It costs 0.5-1.3 LU on a -9 LUFS master. That is the price of a loud master that survives streaming, and you choose when to pay it.

There are 28 factory presets (2.0 added 20), grouped in the menu:

  • MASTER: streaming, Apple Music, hip-hop / R&B, pop, EDM, rock, lo-fi, club, acoustic, podcast, broadcast, mix-bus safety.
  • YOUR STUDIO (BOOM BAP): boom bap streaming master, a loud master that survives the codecs without SAFE, booth rap vocal bus, drum bus knock, beat tape master.
  • VOCAL BUS: peak catcher, upfront, ad-libs / doubles, sung vocal.
  • BEAT / DRUM BUS: drum bus punch, 808 / sub control, trap hard clip, sample-based glue.

Each 2.0 preset was checked on real material (DECISIONS M17), and its description shows as the tooltip of the preset name. User presets and A/B too. Presets never change GAIN: how hard to drive depends on the mix, not the genre.
