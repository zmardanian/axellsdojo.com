MANUAL · VOCAL REVERB + DELAY

Axell Space: getting started and every control

Reverb and delay that step aside for the voice. This page explains every control, with the plugin's own screens. Back to Axell Space.

Getting started

  1. Install. Windows: run the Axell Signature Suite installer (or copy Axell Space.vst3 to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3). Linux: unpack the archive and run ./install.sh.
  2. Rescan. Let your DAW rescan its VST3 plugins; Axell Space appears under the vendor Axell Yobag.
  3. Insert it on the track or bus it is made for, and start from a factory preset close to your material.
  4. Listen, then adjust the few controls described below; A/B compares two settings without losing either.

What it does

The space of the Axell suite: a reverb and a tempo-synced delay, made for vocals.

  • REVERB: BOOTH, ROOM, PLATE and HALL from a 32-line, true-stereo feedback delay network with diffusion inside every loop, three-band decay and early reflections modelled on a real room's geometry. Dense from the first milliseconds, a smooth tail without metallic ringing, decay times that are what the knob says. REAL SPACES (4.0): eight real rooms modelled from their size and walls - booth, bathroom, living room, live room, stairwell, club, chapel, car park - held to their physics. SHAPE: natural, gated or reversed tails.
  • DELAY: synced to the song (1/16 to 1/1, triplets and dotted) or free in milliseconds, exact to the sample; CLEAN, TAPE or ANALOG repeats, wow and flutter, diffusion, feedback, ping-pong, filters, drive, and a send of the repeats into the reverb.
  • ESS DUCK: the suite's sibilance network (from Axell Velvet 3.0) marks every S, SH and CH and keeps it out of the reverb and the delay - no splashy, hissing tails - while the voice itself is untouched.
  • VOICE DUCK: the effects step back while you rap and bloom in the gaps; UNMASK steps them back only in the bands your voice is using at that moment, so the space stays around the words.

More screens: 1/4 throw, ad-lib tail, slap back, 75 % size.

REVERB (2.0: a new core - 32 lines, true stereo, three-band decay, voiced per type; sessions saved before 2.0 keep the 1.0 core, bit for bit, and ENGINE switches)

  • TYPE: BOOTH (a small, dense booth around the voice), ROOM (a natural room, early reflections first), PLATE (the classic vocal plate, dense and bright from the start, no early reflections), HALL (big and long, for hooks).
  • SIZE, DECAY (RT60, 0.2-10 s), PRE-DELAY (0-250 ms: the words stay in front, the space comes behind), BASS (2.0: the lows' decay, 50 - 200 % of DECAY), DAMPING (how much faster the highs die: at 100 % 2.5x faster than the mids in 2.0, 2x in 1.0), FREEZE (2.0: hold the tail, let nothing new in), PRE SYNC (pre-delay in note values of the tempo), EARLY (the early reflections against the tail), MOD (slow movement that keeps the tail smooth; 50 % keeps any pitch wobble under about 7 cents), LOW CUT / HIGH CUT before the reverb, WIDTH (0-200 %), LEVEL.
  • REAL SPACE (4.0): a modelled room instead of the TYPE's algorithm: its own early reflections (true stereo) and a tail decaying per band as the room does; it sets DECAY and SIZE (they step aside). SHAPE (4.0): NATURAL, GATED (open while you rap and for TIME after, then shut) or REVERSE (the tail backwards in segments of TIME: a swell).

DELAY

  • MODE (3.0): CLEAN (exact repeats), TAPE (soft record-head saturation, a head bump, each pass darker, the tape's wow and flutter) or ANALOG (a dark bucket-brigade line, soft clipping); WOW (how much the tape moves: 40 % = a healthy machine, 1.5 cents; 100 % = a worn one, 4 cents) and DIFFUSE (each repeat smears a little more, from echoes to a soft trail, still on the beat).
  • SYNC with a NOTE value at the host's tempo, or TIME in milliseconds (1-2000).
  • FEEDBACK (0-95 %), PING-PONG, LOW CUT / HIGH CUT inside the feedback loop, DRIVE (soft tape-like saturation of the repeats), LEVEL, INTO REVERB (the repeats melt into the space).

DUCKING

  • ESS DUCK with its AMOUNT (0-24 dB): the effects' input is delayed by 8 ms so that the sibilance network can decide before an ess reaches them. The dry voice is not delayed: Space has no latency.
  • VOICE DUCK (0-24 dB) and RELEASE: the effects go down while the voice is loud and come back in its pauses.
  • UNMASK (3.0, 0-100 % = up to 12 dB): the effects step back only where they would cover the voice - per third of an octave, while the voice uses that band - and stay full everywhere else. The display shows what it carves out, live.

OUTPUT

  • MIX: up to 50 % the voice stays at full level and the effects come in; above 50 % the voice goes down; 100 % = wet only, for a send / aux track. At 0 % the output is the input, bit for bit.
  • OUTPUT level, IN / OUT meters, 26 factory presets (vocal plates, booth, rooms, halls, seven real rooms, gated hall, reverse swell, tape slap and echo, throws, ping-pong, analog dub, diffuse trail, ad-lib tails and two 100 %-wet sends), A/B.

Switching the reverb or the delay off lets its tail ring out; changing the type cross-fades; host bypass cross-fades over 10 ms and is then the input, bit for bit.
